# TabScroll 14-day launch plan

## Goal

Reach the first 100 qualified installs, 20 seven-day active users, and 5–10 honest reviews. Do not spend on ads until the store page converts and users return after one week.

## Core message

**Alt-Tab for your Chrome tabs.**

Supporting promise: recognize crowded tabs visually and switch with one shortcut, the mouse wheel, or arrow keys.

## Audience order

1. Developers and QA engineers
2. Researchers and students
3. Recruiters and sales researchers
4. Comparison shoppers and trip planners
5. Self-described tab hoarders

## Schedule

### Days 1–2 — Conversion foundation

- Publish version 0.1.4 with the optimized name and short description.
- Upload the five screenshots from `marketing/store-assets/output/`.
- Add the landing page, support URL, and branded privacy-policy URL.
- Verify that the permission explanation matches actual behavior.

### Days 3–5 — Private seed group

- Invite 20 people who genuinely use many tabs.
- Ask each person to use TabScroll for three work sessions.
- Ask four questions: Was the shortcut memorable? Was switching faster? Did the Chrome permission warning worry you? What would make you uninstall?
- Fix the two most repeated problems.
- Ask satisfied users for an honest review; never pay or reward reviews.

### Days 6–8 — First public launch

- Publish one Reddit post in the most relevant community.
- Stay available for replies for two hours after posting.
- Publish the 15-second demo on YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, and X.
- Record which hook creates store visits: “Alt-Tab for tabs,” “stop clicking favicons,” or “20+ tabs.”

### Days 9–11 — Builder communities

- Publish Show HN.
- Share the technical and privacy story on Indie Hackers.
- Post the LinkedIn version from the maker's personal account.
- Contact five small Chrome/productivity YouTube creators with the demo and a one-sentence pitch.

### Days 12–14 — Improve, then repeat

- Compare landing-page clicks with Chrome Web Store installs.
- Group feedback into permission trust, onboarding, speed, and missing features.
- Update the weakest screenshot or message.
- Publish the best-performing hook again with a different use case.

## Metrics

- Store listing visitors
- Installs
- Listing-to-install conversion
- Active users after 1 and 7 days
- Uninstalls
- Reviews and average rating
- Landing-page CTA clicks by source
- Qualitative permission concerns

## Stop/go rules

- If people visit but do not install, fix listing trust, permissions, and screenshots before adding traffic.
- If people install but do not return, improve onboarding and the core switching flow.
- If one audience retains twice as well as the others, make that audience the next month's primary niche.
- Do not buy ads before at least 20 people have used the extension for a week and the listing has several authentic reviews.
