## Product name

**TabScroll – Visual Tab Switcher**

## Short description

**A fast, private visual tab switcher with scroll, arrow-key, and one-click controls.**

## Detailed description

Too many Chrome tabs to recognize?

TabScroll turns your current window into a focused, full-screen tab switcher. Press `Ctrl+Shift+K` (`Command+Shift+K` on macOS), move with the mouse wheel or arrow keys, then press `Enter` to switch. Prefer the mouse? Click any visible card to open it directly.

### Recognize the right tab

Each card can show the tab title, site address, and favicon. The tab you opened TabScroll from also gets a temporary visual preview. The centered card and nearby context make the next destination clear even when Chrome's tab strip has become a row of tiny icons.

### Fast controls

- Mouse wheel or arrow keys: move through tabs
- Enter: activate the centered tab
- Click any card: switch there immediately
- Escape: close the switcher
- T: switch between light and night themes
- Navigation wraps from the last tab back to the first

### Permission you control

TabScroll installs without access to your full list of tab titles and addresses. On first use, it explains an optional Chrome permission that makes every card recognizable by reading the titles, addresses, and favicons of tabs that are currently open.

Chrome describes that optional capability as access to browsing history because it can reveal the addresses of open tabs. TabScroll uses it only to render the switcher and activate your selection. You can decline and keep using a limited view.

TabScroll does **not** request Chrome's debugger permission, access background-page contents, or capture background-tab screenshots.

### Local by design

- No account or login
- No advertising or profiling
- No analytics, tracking pixels, or third-party telemetry
- No remote server or browsing-data uploads
- Open source with public permission notes and privacy policy

The current tab's temporary preview and open-tab details are processed locally in Chrome. They are not sent anywhere or saved as a browsing-history database.

Chrome internal pages such as `chrome://` pages and some Chrome Web Store pages do not allow extension overlays, so TabScroll cannot open on those protected pages.
